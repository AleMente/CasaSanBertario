Where it is

Cassino: between Rome
and Naples, in the hills

A town of 33,000 in southern Lazio, on the main road and rail line between Italy's two largest cities. Best known internationally for the Abbey of Monte Cassino and the 1944 battles fought around it.

History on the doorstep
The Abbey of Monte Cassino, founded by Saint Benedict in the sixth century, sits ten minutes above the town. The Polish, German and Commonwealth war cemeteries draw visitors from across Europe, North America and Australia year round.
Mountains, parks and coast
The Comino Valley and the Abruzzo, Lazio and Molise National Park — home to the Marsican brown bear and Apennine wolf — begin a short drive away. The ski slopes at Roccaraso and the Tyrrhenian coast are both around an hour by car.
A working town, not a museum
Cassino has its own university and sits within one of central Italy's more active industrial districts. Shops, schools, hospital and station are all within walking distance of the apartment.
